# research/designs-evidence/letter-recode-82ag.md — header treatment & colour use recode under 82a-general (fresh recoder, 2026-09-24)

Scope: `header treatment` and `colour use` ONLY, for all 34 items in `letter-items-l2.csv`, per
`research/82a-general.md` (superseding §4's rows for these two features) and 82a
clarifications 1-4. Admissibility and every other feature are untouched and not looked at.
Source material: `letter-items-l2.csv` preview_url only. The letter/cover-letter corpus .md
files and any agreement file were NOT opened.

**Method.** Each preview was downloaded to `research/designs-evidence/tmp-letrc/` (deleted at
the end of this task) and measured with a scratch PIL script (`tmp-letrc/analyze.py`,
stdlib+Pillow) providing: `grid` (20x20 background sample per B1a), `inkcolors` (full-page scan
for non-background pixels, for locating real chromatic elements per B4 — the whole-page 20x20
grid under-samples thin text/rules and is background-only per the rule), `inkbox` (bounding box
of non-background ink in a y/x band, to locate title/meta block position for split/plain
tests), `findbars`/`rowscan`/`colscan` (full-width rule/band detection), and `box` (median colour
+ dimensions of a region). All numeric values in the tables below are copy-pasted from this
script's output.

**Methodology note (important, found during measurement).** Several preview files (re-encoded
JPEGs, and PNGs plausibly re-saved from JPEG) show a chromatic-pixel artefact around black text:
a faint colour cast (dark red, dark blue, orange) that appears at every line of body text,
scaling with the amount of text on the page, and spread across the whole text area rather than
localised to one graphic. This is compression/antialiasing colour fringe on black glyphs, not
design colour (extends B2's "anti-aliasing... is not colour" to sub-pixel colour fringe, the
print analogue of ClearType fringing). It was checked per case with a pixel bounding-box +
row-histogram (`analyze.py` ad hoc, reported inline where used): fringe is diagnosed when the
colour's hits are spread across most of the page in proportion to text density; a real element is
diagnosed when the hits cluster tightly on one heading/rule/logo. Only clustered hits are counted
toward B4/B5.
